# Process

I began with "How Not to Be Replaced by AI", organised around understanding
AI, identifying its strengths, finding human strengths, and using AI as a
tool. In discussion with Codex, I realised this covered too many loosely
connected questions. I shifted towards the more playful "Surviving Under AI
Rule": a concrete situation in which students would need to make decisions,
rather than receive general advice about the future.

My view of a good course is practical and project-led. Students should
encounter problems, recognise gaps in their understanding, and then
investigate the theory they need. I proposed a reverse timeline: begin in an
AI-governed future, learn to survive there, and return to our present to
consider earlier interventions. My concern about AI's harms and insufficient
oversight motivated the scenario, but I did not regard that explanation as
proven. The future therefore remained explicitly fictional. I also insisted
that the final discussion allow coexistence, ending AI rule, continued AI
governance, or another answer. These directions became the plan and harness
rules
([629a13d](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-nzuuu9/commit/629a13d2ef6c48b19dee06699e47818df01bffa5)).

I used Codex to discuss and organise my ideas into English instructions, and
Claude Code to develop the weekly structure and implement the site. Claude
brought Calling Bullshit into the planning as a reference; its blunt,
provocative framing resonated with the humorous direction I had already
chosen. This was an agent-mediated reference, not a course I independently
studied. I wanted humour to invite curiosity while activities and assessment
remained clear. My proposed first assignment, depicting the AI-governed
world, developed into a dossier revisited through survival planning and a
final argument
([3277928](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-nzuuu9/commit/32779289e3baced971b41e7666e62b195c739892)).

Reviewing the first website sharpened my standards. I wanted the homepage to
explain the subject and assessment immediately and make me want to continue.
Some text felt stiff because I could not understand what it meant, so I
requested connected prose rather than accepting sections that were merely
filled in. I requested a dark opening and typewriter notice for immersion
([a411536](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-nzuuu9/commit/a4115368984ec815d628620c3569ae4394c84c0e)).
When only a panel became dark, I clarified that the whole page should change
([51355ce](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-nzuuu9/commit/51355cec978faef8852a193306b24794bf5438d6)).
Later, I questioned why Lectures contained only two weeks. The navigation was
clarified to distinguish dedicated lecture resources from the complete weekly
sessions
([585e3bb](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-nzuuu9/commit/585e3bb2ac67fd18c86eae9a5ef045ed2b076a63)).

The harness encoded the reverse timeline, separation of evidence and fiction,
clear instructions, and an open ending. Automated checks protected assessment
totals, calendar consistency, linked decks, and preparation materials
([3382613](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-nzuuu9/commit/3382613f0767bfc364bf584be2c1fee844deaa72)).
I deliberately left the final political conclusion unspecified. Judging
whether the prose was understandable or the atmosphere engaging remained
part of my browsing, rather than something passing checks could establish.

That limitation became visible when one Chrome profile showed pale text on a
pale background. Claude corrected theme-handling defects, but the cause of my
specific symptom remained unconfirmed
([8b7ed62](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-nzuuu9/commit/8b7ed62a91629ce620e2292d864699ab5cb1d2c0)).
I deferred further investigation under the deadline rather than claiming
success. This process taught me that directing the agent requires both
explicit rules and personal scrutiny: an implemented feature is not
necessarily a verified student experience.
