**Monday 22 March 2027 — story time: far future, Meridian.**

## The resident's problem

While Priya's appeal works its way through the DRA, her building's water
pressure drops. Three notices arrive about it over two days, and they
don't agree with each other. Nothing in weeks 1–4 prepared you for a
situation where the problem isn't a missing document — it's three
documents that can't all be true.

## Learning goals

By the end of this session you should be able to:

- Distinguish "I can't verify this" from "this is false" — the two are
  not the same claim, and treating them as the same is how uncertainty
  gets guessed away.
- Write a short memo that states what is and isn't known, without
  resolving a genuine contradiction by picking whichever version is more
  convenient.

## Preparation and materials

Read all three before the session — the same building, the same fault,
two days apart:

> NOTICE 1 (Monday, 08:00) — Scheduled maintenance on the block's water
> pressure regulator will cause reduced pressure between 08:00–18:00
> today. No action required.
>
> NOTICE 2 (Monday, 15:00) — Reports of a pressure fault are under
> investigation. This is not a scheduled maintenance event. Residents
> experiencing no water should contact building services directly.
>
> NOTICE 3 (Tuesday, 09:00) — Yesterday's pressure issue has been
> resolved. Scheduled maintenance is complete. Thank you for your
> patience.

## Class activity

In small groups, treat this the way you'd treat any other Archive
material: what, if anything, can you verify independently of the notices
themselves (a resident's own experience of water pressure counts); what's
a reasonable extrapolation (Notice 2 contradicting Notice 1 suggests
Notice 1 was wrong, or misleading, but you can't confirm which); and what
would you need — a source outside CivOp's own notice channel — to settle
it, which you don't currently have. Resist the pull to just believe
Notice 3 because it comes last and sounds resolved.

## Expected output

A short uncertainty memo: two or three sentences on what's confirmed,
what's contradicted, and what evidence would resolve it — addressed to
a resident deciding whether to trust the building's water pressure today.
Guessing an answer to look decisive is the one thing that fails this
brief.
