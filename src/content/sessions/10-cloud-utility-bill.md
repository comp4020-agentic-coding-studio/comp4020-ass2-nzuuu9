**Monday 10 May 2027 — story time: present day.**

## This week's problem

Every allocation decision, appeal, and public notice in Meridian's story
runs on physical infrastructure — power, water for cooling, hardware that
wears out. Nobody in the story has costed it. Before week 11 can propose
an intervention, it needs a real number, or at least a defensible
estimate, of what CivOp actually costs to run.

## Learning goals

By the end of this session you should be able to:

- Use a real published benchmark to produce a grounded estimate for a
  fictional system at a stated scale, rather than inventing a number with
  nothing behind it.
- Clearly separate, in a written brief, which figures are sourced,
  which are extrapolated from a source to a new scale, and which are
  simply assumed for the story to work.

## Preparation and materials

Read before the session: the International Energy Agency's [*Electricity
2024*](https://www.iea.org/reports/electricity-2024) report, specifically
its findings on data centre electricity demand — a real, current source
for what large-scale automated computing infrastructure actually costs in
energy terms, which is the benchmark you'll scale to Meridian's size.

## Class activity

In pairs, estimate CivOp's resource footprint — energy, water for
cooling, hardware refresh — at the scale of a city the size Meridian is
described as (roughly a mid-sized city; state your assumed population).
Start from the IEA report's real figures, extrapolate them to Meridian's
scale, and be explicit in your brief about where a real number ends and
an assumption begins. Do not invent a headline number with no visible
working — every figure needs a one-line note saying whether it's sourced,
extrapolated, or assumed for the story.

## Expected output

A resource-cost brief for CivOp's infrastructure, with every figure
labelled sourced / extrapolated / assumed. This is a required input to
week 11's intervention proposal, not a standalone essay — week 11 has to
use these numbers, not restate the question.
