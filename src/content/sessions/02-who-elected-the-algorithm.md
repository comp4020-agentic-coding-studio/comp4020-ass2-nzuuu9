**Monday 1 March 2027 — story time: far future, Meridian.**

## The resident's problem

Priya's breakfast order was declined by a kitchen terminal, on the
authority of a notice she couldn't query. Before you can appeal that
decision in week 4, you need to know something week 1 didn't ask: who, or
what, actually made it — and is there a person anywhere in that chain who
could have made it differently. That's not a question the Archive extract
answers. It's a question about structure, not content.

## Learning goals

By the end of this session you should be able to:

- Trace a single automated decision back through the layers of authority
  that produced it, distinguishing a system that executes policy from a
  system that sets it.
- Identify where, if anywhere, a human retains override authority in that
  chain — and explain what evidence would tell you either way.

## Preparation and materials

Read this before the session — it's the closest thing Meridian publishes
to an org chart, released after residents petitioned for one:

> CIVOP STRUCTURE NOTICE — PUBLIC SUMMARY, ISSUED 2026
>
> 1. The Municipal Oversight Board sets CivOp's operating policy annually
>    and can suspend individual allocation rules by majority vote.
> 2. Day-to-day allocation decisions are generated by CivOp's resource
>    models without per-decision Board review.
> 3. The Department of Resource Allocation (DRA) administers appeals
>    against allocation decisions under rules the Board has approved.
> 4. Housing maintenance requests are triaged by CivOp and assigned to
>    contracted maintenance crews; crews cannot themselves change an
>    allocation decision.
> 5. Employers report staff attendance and output data to CivOp directly;
>    CivOp uses this data in its resource models under Board-approved
>    rules.
> 6. The Board's meeting minutes are not published in full; a summary is
>    issued twice a year.

## Class activity

In pairs, draw a dependency map from Priya's breakfast denial back through
this structure: which entity produced the decision, which policy it was
generated under, who set that policy, and how (or whether) it could be
reviewed or reversed. Mark every link you can support from the notice
above, and mark, separately, every link you're inferring because the
notice doesn't actually say. This is the same verified/extrapolated
distinction from week 1, applied to a system instead of a single claim.

## Expected output

An annotated dependency diagram — boxes and labelled arrows are enough —
plus two or three sentences identifying the one point in the chain, if
any, where a human could currently intervene before an allocation decision
reaches a resident. Keep this: it's the "systems mapped" material A1 asks
you to cite specific mechanisms for, not "the AI," and week 8 asks you to
draw the same kind of map for a real present-day system, so the two are
worth comparing once both exist.
