**Monday 3 May 2027 — story time: present day.**

## This week's problem

Priya's DRA appeal, back in week 4, existed on paper but had never once
succeeded in practice. That gap — a right to appeal that exists in name
only — isn't unique to Meridian. This week asks you to draft rules for a
real system that close it, rather than merely declare it closed.

## Learning goals

By the end of this session you should be able to:

- Draft an oversight rule that is enforceable — specific enough that
  someone could point to evidence of whether it was followed — rather
  than aspirational.
- Explain why a right of appeal that exists on paper isn't the same as
  one a person could actually use.

## Preparation and materials

Read before the session: Amnesty International's report on the Dutch
childcare benefits scandal, [*Xenophobic Machines: Discrimination Through
Unregulated Use of Algorithms in the Dutch Childcare Benefits
Scandal*](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2021/10/xenophobic-machines-dutch-child-benefit-scandal/)
(Amnesty International, 2021) — a real, documented case where an automated
risk-scoring system wrongly flagged thousands of families for benefit
fraud, with an appeals process that existed on paper and did not, in
practice, catch the error before real harm was done.

## Class activity

In pairs, choose one real, currently deployed automated decision-making
system — a benefits eligibility check, a content-moderation system, a
hiring or tenant-screening tool. Draft three to five oversight rules for
it, each specific enough that a named party could be shown to have
followed or broken it. At least one rule must specify a human-review or
appeal mechanism with a concrete trigger and a concrete timeframe — not
"appeals will be considered," but who reviews what, and by when. For each
rule, write one sentence justifying it against a failure mode from the
Amnesty report or from your own system.

## Expected output

A draft oversight rule set (three to five rules) for your chosen system,
each with a one-sentence justification. This sits between week 8's
governance-lever brief and week 10's resource-cost brief as real-world
material for A3's defended-direction argument.
