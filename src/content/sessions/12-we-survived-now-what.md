**Monday 24 May 2027 — story time: open.**

## This week's problem

There isn't one. That's deliberate. Twelve weeks of Priya's case, one
hinge, and four weeks of real-world grounding don't resolve into a single
correct answer about what should happen to a city like Meridian — or, for
that matter, to the real systems weeks 8–10 asked you to examine. This
week is where you commit to a position anyway, and defend it properly.

## Learning goals

By the end of this session you should be able to:

- State a defensible position on a contested question — continued AI
  governance, reform, ending it outright, or another direction entirely —
  and explain what evidence and reasoning it rests on.
- Fairly state the strongest case against your own position, in terms its
  proponents would recognise as accurate, before answering it.

## Preparation and materials

Bring everything: your [A1 dossier](/assessments/assignment-1/), your
[A2 playbook](/assessments/assignment-2/), your
[week 7 timeline](/sessions/07-we-only-clicked-agree/), and your weeks

synthesising what you've already built, not adding to it.

## Class activity

In small groups, each state your intended A3 position in one sentence,
then take two minutes to argue the strongest case *against* your own
position — not a weak version of it, the version its strongest advocate
would actually make. Your group's job is to tell you honestly whether you
argued it fairly or quietly undersold it. This is the last check before
submission: A3 is marked on whether you engage the strongest
counterargument, not on which position you land on. Coexistence,
continued governance, reform, and dismantling all score identically well
if equally well reasoned and equally well evidenced.
