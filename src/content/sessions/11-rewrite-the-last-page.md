**Monday 17 May 2027 — story time: present day, pre-Compact.**

## This week's problem

Week 7's timeline named the points where Meridian's takeover could still
have gone differently. This week asks you to actually stand at one of
those points and propose what should have happened instead — and to
defend it against people whose job today is to find the hole in it.

## Learning goals

By the end of this session you should be able to:

- Propose an intervention that is feasible given a stated resource
  constraint, not just desirable in the abstract.
- Respond to a specific, substantive critique of your own proposal by
  revising it, rather than defending it unchanged.

## Preparation and materials

Bring your [week 10 resource-cost brief](/sessions/10-cloud-utility-bill/)
and your [week 7 annotated timeline](/sessions/07-we-only-clicked-agree/).
There is no new external material this week — the preparation is making
sure both of those are in a state you can actually use in front of a
partner.

## Class activity

Working from one of week 7's missed intervention points, write a one-
paragraph proposal for what should have happened there instead — a policy
change, a technical constraint, a point of resident leverage exercised
differently. Your proposal must incorporate at least one figure from your
week 10 brief (a resource cost the intervention would need to absorb or
avoid). Pitch it to another pair. They critique it on exactly the grounds
A3 will be marked on: is it feasible given the resources you've costed, and
what's the strongest reason it wouldn't have worked? Revise your proposal
in response before the session ends — the revision is part of the
deliverable, not optional polish.

## Expected output

A revised intervention proposal, incorporating a week 10 resource figure
and a documented response to your peer critique (what was raised, what you
changed or why you didn't). This is the direct basis for A3's requirement
to defend one concrete direction and engage the strongest counterargument
against it.
