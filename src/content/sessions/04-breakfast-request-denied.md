**Monday 15 March 2027 — story time: far future, Meridian.**

## The resident's problem

Priya's Archive record told her the appeal process is "under
development" and that no resident has successfully reversed a
nutritional-allocation decision since it was introduced. Neither of those
facts makes an appeal pointless — they make it a specific, evidence-heavy
task rather than a form-filling exercise. This week you actually do it.

## Learning goals

By the end of this session you should be able to:

- Read a procedural rule set closely enough to identify exactly what
  evidence a decision-maker is required to weigh.
- Draft an appeal that cites specific rules and specific evidence, and a
  fallback plan that doesn't depend on the appeal succeeding.

## Preparation and materials

Read this extract before the session — the rules Priya's appeal, if she
files one, would actually be assessed against:

> DEPARTMENT OF RESOURCE ALLOCATION — APPEAL RULES, EXTRACT 4.2–4.6
>
> 4.2. An appeal must cite the specific notice being contested and state
>      what outcome is sought.
> 4.3. An appeal may include evidence of a data error in the resident's
>      logged activity, household size, or standing account status.
> 4.4. An appeal may not contest the allocation formula itself, only its
>      application to the resident's specific case.
> 4.5. Appeals are reviewed against the resident's Archive record; a
>      resident may submit supporting material not already in that record.
> 4.6. A decision on appeal is final; a resident may resubmit only if new
>      evidence becomes available.

## Class activity

In pairs, work Priya's case through this process in order. First, identify
exactly which rule her breakfast denial was issued under (Archive item 1)
and what 4.4 rules out as a basis for appeal. Second, decide what evidence
under 4.3 she could plausibly gather — a logged activity error, a
household-size discrepancy — and note it's an extrapolation, not a fact,
until she checks it. Third, draft the appeal itself: the notice cited, the
outcome sought, the evidence attached. Fourth, and only after the appeal
draft exists, write one paragraph on what Priya does if it's refused —
this is the fallback plan, and it has to work without the appeal.

## Expected output

Three linked artefacts: the appeal draft (rule cited, evidence attached),
a one-line note on what 4.4 rules out and why, and the fallback plan. Keep
all three — this is exactly the case work **A2 — Survival Playbook** is
required to extend in week 6, not redo.
