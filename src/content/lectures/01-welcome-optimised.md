## Welcome

This course is a survival guide dressed up as a civics course — or maybe
it's the other way around. Either way, it's set in a city already governed
by an AI system, and we're going to tell that city's story backwards.

CivOp — Civic Operations — is Meridian's governing administrative
platform: the system that allocates food, water, power, and housing
maintenance, and answers, or doesn't, when a resident asks why. It didn't
arrive all at once. It arrived the way most infrastructure does — one
consolidation, one efficiency gain, one reasonable-sounding vote at a
time. That's most of what this course is actually about.

## Why the story runs backwards

We start in a settled future, where CivOp already runs everything and
residents have mostly made their peace with it. From there the story
moves backwards to the moment of takeover — the hinge, in week 7 — and
then forward again into the present day, where the decisions that led to
Meridian are still being made, right now, by people who are not
fictional. Week 12 ends the story open: whether Meridian's ending was
inevitable is the last thing you'll be asked to argue.

The teaching calendar doesn't run backwards — Monday still follows
Monday, in order, all semester. Only the story does. Keep the two
separate; the homepage timeline exists for exactly that reason.

## How to read an Archive extract

Most weeks hand you a document from inside Meridian's world — an Archive
extract, a notice, a record — and ask you to sort its claims into three
bins: **verified** (you could check this against something outside the
document itself), **extrapolated** (a reasonable guess from something
verifiable, but not itself confirmed), and **fictional** (invented, with
nothing behind it). None of it should be taken at face value on the
strength of how official it sounds — including, for what it's worth, this
lecture.

## Where this is going

All three assessments extend one running dossier on Priya's case, each
building on whatever you produce in class that week. What each assessment
actually requires is covered when it comes due, not before — for now,
it's enough to know the sessions add up to something, rather than each
standing alone.
