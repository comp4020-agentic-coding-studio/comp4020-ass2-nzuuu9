## The hinge

Every week so far has shown you what it's like to live inside CivOp's
decisions. This lecture is the one week that steps outside Priya's case
entirely to ask a structural question: how does a city end up governed
this way, and where — precisely, not vaguely — could it have gone
differently?

## What's in the deck

The [slide deck](/decks/week-07/) is this week's actual teaching material;
read it before or during the session rather than treating this page as a
substitute. It works through the difference between a takeover and a
transition, walks an annotated timeline of Meridian's six to eight key
inflection points (each tagged fact, extrapolation, or fiction, with a
real-world analogue placed alongside it), excerpts the Compact — the
clickwrap terms residents technically agreed to — and closes by asking
who held leverage at each missed intervention point, and why they didn't
use it.

## Before the session

There's no separate reading this week beyond the deck itself. Come having
gone through it once; the [class activity](/sessions/07-we-only-clicked-agree/)
asks you to extend its timeline, not repeat it back.

## Where this goes

This lecture is the pivot the rest of the course turns on. Weeks 8–11 take
the real-world analogues raised here and examine them directly, and A3
asks you to argue, using this timeline as evidence, whether Meridian's
ending was really inevitable.
