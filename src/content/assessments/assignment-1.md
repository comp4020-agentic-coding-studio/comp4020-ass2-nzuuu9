## The brief

> Write a sourced profile of one Meridian resident's ordinary day under
> CivOp: what they can access, what is denied, and why.

This is not a character sketch. It is a dossier — the kind of document a
caseworker, journalist, or the resident themselves might actually compile
to understand their own situation. Every factual claim about how Meridian
works has to be traceable to something: the [CivOp structure
notice](/sessions/02-who-elected-the-algorithm/), the [DRA appeal
rules](/sessions/04-breakfast-request-denied/), or another Archive-style
document from weeks 1–3. Where you extend beyond what those documents
state, say so explicitly — extrapolation is allowed and often necessary,
but it has to be labelled, not presented as established fact.

The room this brief leaves is the resident's story itself: who they are
beyond the bureaucratic record, what their day actually looks like, which
denial or restriction the dossier centres on. Weeks 1–3 gave you Priya
Osei-Lindqvist's case in detail; you may write her dossier, or invent a
different resident of Meridian and build their case with the same rigour,
so long as every mechanism you describe is consistent with the source
material already established in the course.

## What you submit

A single dossier document (prose, with a supporting diagram or table if
useful — your week 2 dependency map is a reasonable starting point).
Every claim about how Meridian works must carry a tag: **verified**
(directly stated in a course source document), **extrapolated** (a
reasonable inference from a verified claim, clearly marked as such), or
**fictional** (world detail that doesn't map to a real mechanism but is
needed for the story to make sense). A dossier with no tags, or with tags
applied inconsistently, cannot be marked on the sourcing criterion at all.

## How it's marked

The table above shows the weighting. Two things worth stating directly:
"systems mapped" means naming the specific mechanism responsible for each
denial or restriction (a named CivOp resource model, a specific DRA rule,
a specific Board decision) — a dossier that explains everything by
gesturing at "the algorithm" will score poorly here regardless of how well
it's written. Comedic tone and entertainment value are not assessed
either way.
