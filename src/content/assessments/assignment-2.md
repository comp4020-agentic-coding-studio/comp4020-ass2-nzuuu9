## The brief

> Write a contingency plan for your A1 resident or household facing a
> named service failure or resource denial, and show that you tested it.

A playbook that has never been tested against anything is just a list of
hopes. This assignment extends your A1 dossier: the same resident, the
same household, now facing a concrete failure — the kind covered in
[week 4's appeal process](/sessions/04-breakfast-request-denied/),
[week 5's contradictory notices](/sessions/05-definitely-authentic/), or
[week 6's outage scenario](/sessions/06-system-offline/). State the
scenario precisely enough that a marker can check your plan against it,
then show at least one place where testing the plan against that scenario
made you change it. A plan that survived first contact unchanged is
either trivial or wasn't actually tested — say which, and why, if that's
genuinely the case.

The room this brief leaves is the scenario itself and how far the plan
goes: whether it leans on institutional channels (an appeal, a CivOp
continuity guarantee), informal ones (a resident network, mutual aid), or
a combination, and how it survives the specific failure mode you choose to
test it against.

## What you submit

A contingency plan document, addressed to the resident it's for — someone
who might genuinely need to follow it under stress. Name the failure
scenario explicitly near the top. Include a short, honest account of what
you tested it against and what you changed as a result; this is graded
material, not an appendix.

## How it's marked

See the table above. "Continuity with dossier" means a marker who read
your A1 should recognise this as the same case continued, not a fresh
scenario with the same character's name attached. "Testing and revision"
requires an actual documented change, not an assertion that the plan is
already robust. Comedic tone and entertainment value are not assessed
either way.
